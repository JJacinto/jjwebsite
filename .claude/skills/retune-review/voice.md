# João's voice

Drawn from the copy João wrote for this site. When unsure, reread the real pages (`src/pages/about.astro`, `index.astro`, `contact.astro`) and match them.

## How he sounds

- **First person, plain, specific.** He says what he did and what happened: "Tripled weekly webinar volume after shipping the customisation feature." Not "drove significant growth".
- **Concrete over abstract.** Real names, numbers and tools: "a 40-step admin flow into a single configuration step", "themes for Hi5 and MySpace", "Fullstory, Mixpanel, and Looker".
- **Honest, a bit dry.** "Most of the time I wasn't, not entirely." He admits limits and doesn't oversell.
- **Short sentences carry the point.** "Shipped isn't done." "That's the loop." Use these sparingly. One per paragraph at most, and only when it lands.
- **Talks to a peer, not a recruiter.** Like explaining his work to another senior designer over coffee.
- **British/European spelling where he already uses it:** customisation, optimise. Keep whatever the surrounding copy uses.

## Banned words and phrases

Never use these in copy you write or rewrite. If a comment asks to remove AI jargon, also look for these in the surrounding text he pointed at.

load-bearing, leverage, seamless/seamlessly, delve, robust, elevate, unlock, empower, harness, foster, streamline, supercharge, unleash, navigate (as a metaphor), landscape, realm, tapestry, holistic, synergy, innovative, cutting-edge, game-changer, transformative, impactful, meaningful, pivotal, crucial, testament, north star, deep dive, end-to-end (as filler), best-in-class, world-class, "at the end of the day", "in today's fast-paced world", "it's worth noting", "passionate about", "I'm on a mission to"

## Patterns that read as AI

Rewrite these even when no banned word appears:

- **"It's not X, it's Y" / "Not X — Y."** One on the whole page is enough. Usually just say Y.
- **Lists of exactly three** in sentence after sentence ("clear, calm, and confident"). Use the number of items you actually have.
- **Em-dash chains.** One dash per sentence at most. Prefer a full stop or a comma.
- **A punchy summary line closing every paragraph.** Keep the one that matters and cut the rest.
- **Inflated abstractions** like "operational weight", "the hard part was solved before they arrived". Say the concrete thing.
- **Rhetorical questions and colon reveals** ("The secret? Simplicity.").
- **Hedging filler:** "truly", "really", "incredibly", "deeply".

## Checklist before shipping a line

1. Would João say it out loud to a colleague without wincing?
2. Is there a concrete noun, number or example in it?
3. Zero banned words?
4. Same facts as before, nothing invented?
5. Roughly the same length, so the layout holds?
